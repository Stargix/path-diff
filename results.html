Evaluation details

The current bank compares trained systems, not isolated policy architectures. Results below retain every original attempt and separate task profiles and requested speeds. All are development evaluations in simulation, with three online runs per controller.

Metrics and experimental scope

Original task pass
The recorded task predicate combines first terminal arrival, terminal position/yaw/height, achieved route-average speed and a spatial average-height criterion. Posture scoring excludes ±0.25 m around changes and accepts height MAE up to 4 cm. Passing does not establish that every intermediate posture was reached.
Tracking-gated pass
Original task pass plus active lateral CTE RMSE ≤10 cm. We preserve the historical labels and expose the added tracking requirement instead of calling both scores simply “success”.
Arrival and timing
Arrival means the terminal-task arrival event used by the task evaluator. It is distinct from the route-progress arrival diagnostic. The export now retains both fields separately. Arrival mean-speed error uses the task's first terminal arrival, never reaching the endpoint early and waiting. The historical mean-speed tolerance of 0.08 m/s is absolute; it is not an equal relative timing tolerance across requested speeds or a strict deadline.
Active tracking errors
The main table averages each episode's temporal CTE RMSE and temporal height MAE over actors. Each trace ends at arrival, failure or timeout. Failed episodes can cover less distance; a low partial-trajectory error is not equivalent to completing the route accurately. Initialization drift during policy-controlled settling remains included, so the table does not isolate steady-state tracking.
All-section diagnostic
A separate retrospective check requires spatial height MAE ≤4 cm in each requested section, including transition response. Samples use first passage on a 1 cm grid. The first 4 cm and last 15 cm lie outside the coverage domain; one missing initial grid cell is tolerated for post-step logging. Missing internal or terminal coverage fails the check and no height is imputed. These conventions are exploratory, not independently validated task tolerances.
Height-response plots
Within each actor, recorded route responses are averaged at spatial locations. Curves then average available actor means; shading spans their minimum and maximum, not a confidence interval. Counts can decrease where trajectories end. The single-change subset uses 0.25 m/s and the repeated subset 0.35 m/s, on base-family routes only. Mean curves do not describe every execution.
What is not measured yet
The present figures do not establish physical smoothness, safety, controllable intermediate heights or a causal benefit of multimodal modelling. Those claims need synchronized response and motion measurements, with successful transitions distinguished from delayed, partial or omitted changes.
Different systems and support
The frozen-expert hierarchy decides at 5 Hz. Path Diff and Gaussian BC + PPO replan at 12.5 Hz; joint targets are applied at 50 Hz. Expert access, data coverage, training recipes and checkpoint selection differ. Diffusion and hierarchy checkpoints were historically selected; Gaussian uses fixed-budget final checkpoints. Three diffusion online runs share an offline prior.
Separate sources of evidence
The imitation/refinement and temporal-input tables come from the original I2R experiments. They are not pooled with the newer comparative bank. Comparison videos are new closed-loop rollouts with their own capture metadata, not exact replays of the trajectories used for aggregate tables.

Reference: PPO without BC initialization

Three random-initialized Gaussian PPO runs were also evaluated under the existing refinement setup. They use demonstration-derived normalization and are not a demonstration-free RL baseline. Their failure under this recipe does not establish that RL from scratch cannot solve the task.
